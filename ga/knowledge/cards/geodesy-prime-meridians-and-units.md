Longitude was counted from the national observatory before Greenwich was adopted in 1884, and on many series for
decades after: Paris (2 deg 20' 14" E of Greenwich; French, Belgian and some Ottoman sheets into the 20th century),
Ferro (17 deg 40' W, a convention rather than the island; Austrian, German, Spanish and central European sheets),
Madrid (3 deg 41' 16" W), Pulkovo (30 deg 19' 42" E; Russian and Soviet sheets), Washington (77 deg 03' 56" W; some
US sheets of the 19th century), Rome Monte Mario (12 deg 27' 08" E), Lisbon, Stockholm, Copenhagen, Oslo, Athens,
Batavia. A sheet whose read longitudes fit only after a constant offset of a few degrees is on one of these; the
offset names the meridian, and the margin usually says. Lengths: the chain (66 feet, 20.1168 m) and its 100 links
on British, Irish, American and colonial surveys ("4 chains to an inch" is 1:3,168), the furlong (10 chains), the
statute mile (1,609.344 m), the Irish and Scots miles and chains (longer: an Irish mile is 2,240 yards), the toise
(1.949 m) and the lieue on French maps before the metre, local feet across the German states, the verst (1,066.8 m)
on Russian sheets, the US survey foot against the international foot (two parts per million, irrelevant below 100
km). Scales stated in "inches to the mile": one inch is 1:63,360, two inch 1:31,680, six inch 1:10,560, 25 inch is
1:2,500 (25.344 inches), and 1:500 is 10.56 feet to the mile. Read the unit off the scale bar, never assume metres.
