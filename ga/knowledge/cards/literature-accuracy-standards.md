National Map Accuracy Standards (USA, 1947): for scales larger than 1:20,000, 90% of well-defined points within
1/30 inch at map scale; smaller scales, 1/50 inch; this is the origin of the half-millimetre rule of thumb. NSSDA
(1998): report accuracy as RMSE from independent check points and convert to a 95% circular error by 1.7308 x RMSE
(and 90% by 1.5175 x). ASPRS (2014): accuracy classes by RMSE in ground units, with checkpoints well distributed
and at least twenty of them. ISO 19157: positional accuracy as a data-quality element with a stated evaluation
method. Producers' stated accuracies, approximately: Ordnance Survey large-scale topography about 0.4 m RMSE in
urban (1:1,250 survey), 1.1 m rural (1:2,500), 4 m mountain and moorland (1:10,000); national 1:25,000 series a
few metres; OSM positional accuracy varies by place from metres to tens of metres with no stated figure; a
century-old series was drawn to the standard of its day, about the half-millimetre, with datum and projection on
top. Use these to state the floor of any accuracy claim and to size the check-point set: a claimed 2 m RMSE from
four points on a 1:10,560 sheet is not a measurement.
