Stop when a placement is accepted by the policy with its second family, when every tool that could add a new family
has run, or when the cost cap is reached. Deliver one of three things: an accepted placement with its probability,
evidence and cost; a candidate placement marked for review with what is missing (which family did not agree, or
was not available); or an abstention with the reason (no cues, no candidate area, no fit, no truth). Do not keep
running matchers on a sheet whose reading found nothing to place: a planner's value on graded drawings was knowing
when to stop (4 steps on sheets with nothing to place) rather than extra placements; it wasted steps where tool
contracts were too coarse. In a batch, stop the pilot when its placement is accepted or the pilot's families are
exhausted; run the check sheets with the frozen recipe and stop each by the same rule; a recipe whose pilot was not
accepted is not frozen and the batch is delivered as pilot-failed with the reason.
