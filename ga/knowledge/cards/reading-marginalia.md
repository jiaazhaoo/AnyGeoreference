The margin of a printed map is metadata written by the surveyor. Title: the area and the series (which gives the
index to look up). Scale: a representative fraction (1:25,000), a statement (six inches to one mile), or a bar
(measure its pixel length against its stated length to get metres per pixel directly). Edition and survey dates:
which epoch of reference to match against (a 1905 revision matches the 1900 road network, not today's). Projection
and datum notes on sheets after about 1920 ("Transverse Mercator projection, OSGB36 datum"; "Polyconic projection,
North American datum"): which plane to fit in and what shift to expect. Grid notes: the zone, the false origin,
the grid-to-true-north convergence and the magnetic declination with its date and annual change; a north arrow may
show three norths, use grid north for a projected fit and true north for a graticule fit. Sheet number and adjoining
sheet diagram: the series position and the neighbours (a family for verification). Legend: the symbology, which
tells the structure matcher what a road or a railway looks like on this sheet. Publisher and printer: the archive to
search for a georeferenced copy. None of this is a control point; all of it narrows the search and chooses the fit.
