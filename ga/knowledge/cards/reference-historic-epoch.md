A sheet surveyed in 1890 shows the 1890 world; matching it against today's network succeeds on what persisted and
fails on what did not, and the matcher must know which is which. Persists for a century or more: railways (even
closed lines leave embankments and the modern layer often keeps them as paths), canals, rivers (except where
straightened), parish and county boundaries, churches and churchyards, market places, bridges, main roads between
towns, field boundaries in enclosed countryside, the street grid of a town centre that was not bombed or cleared.
Changes: suburbs (absent on the old sheet, present now: no penalty, missing structure is not contradicting
structure), bypasses and ring roads (present now, absent then), cleared slum streets and renamed streets, docks
and reclaimed shore, quarries, airfields, reservoirs that drowned villages. Practice: weight persistent classes
higher in structure and name matching; treat "present on reference, absent on sheet" as neutral and "present on
sheet, absent on reference" as mild evidence against, since the old sheet has nothing that never existed; prefer a
historical reference when one exists (a georeferenced older edition, a historical gazetteer); report the sheet's
date next to the reference's date in the evidence.
