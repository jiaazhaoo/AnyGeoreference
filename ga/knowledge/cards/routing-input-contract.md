The recommended input is three roughly similar sheets (same series, same style, same era); one sheet is accepted.
With three, the batch profile says what is common (cue kinds every sheet has) and what varies (a north arrow on two,
a scale bar on one); the recipe is composed for the common part and the variation decides the fallbacks. The pilot
runs on one sheet with whatever truth is available; the check runs the frozen recipe on the other two and compares
placements across sheets: adjacent sheets must tile, a road must continue, the scale must agree. That agreement is
an evidence family one sheet alone cannot give. Sheets that are not similar (a town plan and an aerial photo) are two
batches, not one; a profile whose cue kinds share nothing across sheets says so and splits. A batch is also the unit
of cost: one plan, three readings, one build of a missing primitive if needed, one evaluation.
